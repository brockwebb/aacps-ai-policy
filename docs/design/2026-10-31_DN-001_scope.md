# DN-001: scope, anchors, prior art and schema reuse

**Date:** 2026-10-31. **Task:** AP-01 Part A. **Status:** decided (grounded; operator overrides if wrong).

## 1. Scope statement

The corpus is: the draft AACPS Board Policy DM, Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence (October 2026); the public documents it
cites; the public documents that govern it (Maryland State Department of Education guidance and model policy, federal student-privacy
and child-online-safety regulation, the AACPS policies and regulations it references); and the public meeting records of its adoption.
Nothing else. No student or staff data, no personal data, nothing that cannot be fetched or lawfully redistributed (CLAUDE.md rules). A
document declined for access or licence still gets a manifest entry with the reason.

Process facts that bound the output (observed on https://www.aacps.org/page/board-policies, 2026-10-31): the draft is open for comment
through **2026-11-21**; comments go to policycomments@aacps.org; they apply to the policy and any accompanying regulation, not to
supporting documents; the Board may edit comments to exclude obscene language or confidential information. The comment therefore has
three weeks from the date of this note.

## 2. Anchors, not questions

Per squiddy DN-028 the comment's questions are not the input to the graph. The graph is built to support four anchor tests, which are
properties of any policy text and do not presuppose a finding:

1. **Definitional consistency:** a term defined in the draft and defined (or used undefined) in a document it cites or is bound by.
2. **Uncited claims:** an assertion of fact or authority in the draft with no reference.
3. **Obligations without mechanism:** an obligation with no stated responsible party, process, timeline or consequence.
4. **Conflicts** between the draft and the documents it cites or is bound by.

## 3. Prior art (read before building; CLAUDE.md doctrine section 1)

- **fss-policy-kg** (`../icsp_notebook`): the schema this reuses. Document, Definition, Obligation, Reference, Conflict, with
  CONFLICTS_WITH adjudicated at a stamped criteria version, `docs/adding_a_document.md` for admission. Adopted for the decomposition;
  its `kg/config.yaml` definition-heading patterns are federal-statute-shaped and may not fit a board policy (to be tested in AP-02).
- **hazbrief-mcp** `graphs/hazbrief-corpus/graph.yaml`: the shape of a squiddy consumer spec. Adopted: intake, `policy: public_only`,
  extraction closed. Departed from: the claim overlay, concept vocabularies and extraction roles are hazmat-corpus choices with
  a downstream test of their own; none is carried over.
- **Public work on obligation/conflict graphs** (web search, standard mode, 2026-10-31, one query: "knowledge graph policy documents
  obligations definitions conflict detection deontic extraction legal norms"). The field is named: deontic modality extraction from
  regulatory text (GDPR and DSA pipelines using LLMs plus a graph for cross-references), norm conflict identification (two norms
  regulating the same act with opposed modality; Univ. of Luxembourg "Detecting Deontic Conflicts in Dynamic Settings", a 2008 Purdue
  CERIAS privacy-regulation paper on modality and negated-concept inconsistency), and violation reification as graph nodes. This
  supports the fss decomposition (force on the Obligation, reified Conflict with a criteria version) and tells us conflict detection
  is opposed modality over the same act or term, not text similarity. **This is one search, not a literature review**: the papers were
  not read, only their abstracts seen in results; AP-02 should read two before the extraction prompt is written. No search was run for
  education-policy-specific or school-AI-policy comparison work.
- **MSDE model local board policy** (`msde-model-local-board-policy-ai` in the candidates): the closest prior art for the draft itself. If
  Policy DM derives from it, the useful comparison is departures from the model, which the graph supports as Definition and Obligation
  alignment. Not read yet.

## 4. Schema reuse decision

| fss-policy-kg | here | why |
|---|---|---|
| Document | kit `Work` | the kit's admitted-source class; same role |
| Reference | kit `ExternalReference` | the kit's class for a cited item not held |
| Obligation | `Obligation` (declared in graph.yaml) | fits the federal schema unchanged: text, deontic force, who it binds, section path, page |
| Definition | `Definition` (declared) | term and text |
| Conflict | `Conflict` node plus `ConflictsWith` edge (declared) | fss has CONFLICTS_WITH as an edge overlay; reified so a verdict carries its reason and criteria version, as the prior art on reified violations does |

No new class beyond the five. The declarations are written per graph because the kit's base schema lacks them; filed as
`../squiddy/cc_tasks/2026-10-31_SQ-AP01c_*`. `Obligation.binds` and `Obligation.force` are free strings in this spec; fss's closed
force vocabulary (prohibition, obligation, permission, recommendation) is not enforced by the schema and should be before extraction.
